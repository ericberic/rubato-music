# Danielsen et al. — Timing Of Compound Musical Sounds

## Source

Anne Danielsen, Justin London, Martin Torvik Langerød, and Guilherme Schmidt
Câmara, “All About That Bass Drum? Asynchrony Effects on the Perceived Timing
of Compound Musical Sounds,” *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences*,
2026. DOI: [10.1111/nyas.70306](https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.70306).

## Date Read

2026-08-03.

## Why It Matters

Rubato needs evidence for when a delayed spatial layer changes perceived timing
or separates into another event. This study directly tests instrument-pair
asynchrony and attack character instead of treating latency tolerance as a
single constant.

## Key Facts

- Participants aligned a probe to compound instrument sounds whose components
  varied from 0 to plus/minus 80 ms of asynchrony.
- Fast-attack instruments strongly pulled the compound sound's perceived timing
  center.
- Fast/slow-attack pairs could retain one perceived timing center anchored to
  the fast sound.
- Two fast-attack sounds showed evidence of perceptual segregation at
  asynchronies of 40 ms or more in the tested stimuli.
- Perceived event time can occur from a few to more than 100 ms after acoustic
  onset, depending on the sound.

## Caveats

- The stimuli were selected instrument pairs, not Chopin orchestra stems in
  Eric's room.
- The tested offsets do not validate a universal threshold for Rubato.
- Perceived unity and acceptable accompaniment timing are related but different
  judgments.
- Renderer timbre, level, register, room position, and musical role remain
  project-specific variables.
